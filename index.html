01 · Same incumbent, two elections

She kept about two-thirds of her 2020 vote

2020 was a presidential year, so turnout was way up. Colom got fewer votes than Espy in every county. Hyde-Smith got fewer than her own 2020 number in every county too. Hers just fell further.

What county totals can't tell you
Whether a 2020 Hyde-Smith voter stayed home, voted Colom, or voted Pinkins. All three look identical in the totals. Sorting that out takes voter-file or survey data. There's some survey data in section 08.

06 · The count

The late ballots didn't save her

Election night was basically a tie. The bet on the Republican side was that absentees and affidavits would pull her back ahead. They went the other way.

Affidavit ballots vs regular ballots

Regular or overallAffidavit ballots

Only counties that reported both are charted. Oktibbeha affidavits went 401 Colom, 96 Hyde-Smith, 5 Pinkins. Lafayette's went 69 to 29 and Chickasaw's about 55 points Colom, but nobody published their regular-ballot split. Statewide, 12,767 affidavits had been processed by Nov 8, about 77% accepted.

08 · Survey estimates

The survey numbers. Models, not counts.

These are the New York Times voter estimates and the Voter Poll. They're the only thing that breaks the vote down by race and age. Treat them as direction.

  • Black voters went 91 to 7 Colom.
  • White voters went Hyde-Smith by 49. Trump won them by 68 in 2024.
  • Under 30 went 62 to 33 Colom. That's a 44-point swing from 2024.
  • Black share of the electorate was about 33%, up from 30% in 2024. Black Mississippians are about 36% of eligible voters.
  • Strength In Numbers ran this year's vote choices through 2024's electorate and got Hyde-Smith by about 4.
Plug those group numbers back in and you get Hyde-Smith up about 3, not Colom up 0.3. So they're off somewhere. The county returns aren't modeled. They're just counts.
